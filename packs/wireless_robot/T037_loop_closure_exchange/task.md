## Prompt

### Scenario

Four mobile robots traverse an obstructed indoor site while a shared wireless channel connects them to a loop-closure verification service. Each robot produces local landmark observations and a drifting pose estimate. A compact metadata broadcast can reveal a possible overlap with another robot, but geometric verification consumes a larger packet and must wait in a finite service queue. A correct inter-robot loop closure reduces the two robots' pose drift; an accepted false match increases it. The robots must still navigate to their individual goals before their scenario deadlines. Bandwidth, packet loss, propagation delay and queue waiting therefore affect both map consistency and physical task completion.

At every action boundary, the same controller chooses one legal motion for every robot, decides whether each robot attempts to broadcast the observation produced by that motion, and may request at most one currently visible candidate edge for verification. The fixed simulator then admits packets, evaluates deterministic channel outcomes, resolves safe motion, creates post-motion observations, advances the service queue, applies completed loop closures and drift, and exposes the resulting feedback at the next boundary. The controller must not use a future observation, future candidate edge, future queue result or future hash sample.

### Research Problem

Build a deterministic simulator and a unified state-driven method for all fifteen canonical scenario variants. Decide when metadata is worth its one-unit cost, which currently visible cross-robot candidate should receive a one-time geometric verification request, and how each robot should move while overlap ambiguity, communication budget, queue delay, drift correction and navigation deadlines interact. A deterministic heuristic, search, planner or optimization method is allowed, but machine learning and training are prohibited. The method should explain how its choices balance successful loop closures against false matches, packet load, service delay and motion safety.

### Experimental Setting and Constraints

#### Canonical evaluation units

There are five fixed scenarios and variants `v=0,1,2`. The canonical key is `scenario=<name>|variant=<v>`, giving fifteen units. Each unit has action slots `t=0,...,79` and state boundaries `b=0,...,80`; boundary `0` contains the listed starts, energy `100` for every robot, zero drift, no received metadata, no candidate edge, an empty validation queue and no graph edge. Boundary `80` is processed for arrivals and service but has no action.

#### State, observations and decisions

At action-start boundary `t`, each robot has its true cell, remaining energy, two-dimensional local drift, its own past action and packet outcomes, and the global metadata/candidate-edge information that has successfully arrived by that boundary. A robot may use its current local pose estimate (true cell plus current drift), fixed geometry, already received metadata, unexpired candidate edges with their public similarity and deadline, completed verification feedback and queue history. It may not use any observation generated by the current action, an unreceived metadata packet, a future candidate, a future service result or a future hash sample.

Each action decision contains exactly `move`, `broadcast_metadata` and `validate_edge`, alongside its required `slot` and `robot` indices. `move` is one of `stay,N,E,S,W`. `broadcast_metadata=true` requests a one-unit metadata packet for the observation generated after this robot's move; the packet content is therefore not available when the decision is made. `validate_edge` is `null` or one public candidate edge ID visible at this action-start boundary. At most one verification request is made by each robot in a slot. Any robot may request a visible edge, but an edge may be requested at most once in the entire unit. The same procedure must generate all records; using scenario, variant, robot or absolute slot as a lookup into a pre-filled action table is not a valid method.

For robot `i`, a post-motion observation at slot `t` uses the cell reached at boundary `t+1` and the drift that existed before the end-of-slot drift increment. A landmark `j` is visible exactly when its Euclidean distance from that cell is at most `3`. Let `d=clip(distance/3,0,1)`, `sigma=0.10+0.05v`, and multiply `sigma` by `(0.8,1,1.3,1.6)` for robots `r0..r3` only in `heterogeneous_drift`. Its quality is `q=clip(1-0.10*sigma-0.03*d,0,1)`. The fixed descriptor is a length `4L` vector, where `L` is the number of landmarks in the scenario: for every landmark ID in ascending order append `(visible_flag, relative_x/3, relative_y/3, q)` and append zeros for an invisible landmark. The local pose estimate is the post-motion cell plus current drift. The observation ID is `scenario=<name>|variant=<v>|robot=<i>|slot=<t>`.

#### Metadata, candidate edges and verification

A successful metadata packet arriving at boundary `t+1` reveals to every robot its observation ID, descriptor, visible landmark IDs, post-motion local pose estimate and the mean coordinate of its visible landmarks (the anchor; `(0,0)` when none are visible). The descriptor distance between two received observations is the Euclidean distance of their fixed vectors, and `similarity=clip(1-distance/2,0,1)`. A candidate edge is created at the first boundary at which both metadata records are available, only when the robots differ, their visible-landmark intersection is non-empty and `similarity>=0.55`. The unordered pair is canonicalized lexicographically; its ID is `edge=<obs_id_a>--<obs_id_b>`. An edge is generated only once, expires after its generation boundary plus `10` (strictly later boundaries cannot request it), and is removed after a verification request or expiry.

For a requested edge, let `delta_hat` be the difference of the two received local pose estimates and `delta_meta` the difference of their anchors. The fixed geometric residual is `r=norm(delta_hat-delta_meta)`. The edge is geometrically consistent when the shared landmark set is non-empty and `r<=0.35`. A verification packet has payload `4`. After an admitted packet's channel transmission succeeds, define the separate verification sample as `u_validate=uint64(SHA256(UTF-8("<scenario>|<variant>|<edge_id>|validate|<17v>"))[:16])/2^64`; the service result is accepted only when `u_validate < 0.95-0.05v`, otherwise it is a rejected verification with no graph change. If accepted and geometrically consistent, it is a correct loop closure: the two endpoint drifts are multiplied by `0.62`, and the edge with residual `r` is added to the pose graph. If accepted but inconsistent, it is a false closure: `(0.08,0.08)` is added to both endpoint drifts and the edge with residual `r` is recorded. A verification whose transmission fails, is rejected by the queue, or expires produces no graph edge. The result of a completed packet is visible only at its completion boundary.

#### Communication, motion and event order

Each successful metadata request uses `1` payload unit and each verification request uses `4`. The shared channel has `12` units per slot, except `6` on `budget_burst` slots `24..35`; the total accepted payload budget per variant is `640`. Requests are sorted by `(generation_slot, packet_kind, owner_robot, edge_id)`, where `packet_kind` is `0` for metadata and `1` for verification; complete packets are admitted while both capacity and remaining budget permit, and a packet is never split. The channel success probability for an admitted request is `p=clip(0.93-0.04*d_link-0.06*b-0.025*(n-1),0.10,0.93)`, where `d_link` is the action-start distance to `(11,9)` divided by `sqrt(21^2+17^2)`, `b=1` only for the burst slots, and `n` is the one-based position among admitted requests in that slot. For packet ID `tx=<scenario>|<variant>|<kind>|<owner>|<generation>|<edge_id-or-none>`, `u=uint64(SHA256(UTF-8(tx+"|salt="+str(17v)))[:16])/2^64`; it succeeds exactly when `u<p`. There are no implicit retries.

Successful metadata arrives at the next boundary. A successful verification arrives at the next boundary and enters a FIFO of capacity `8`; if full it is dropped. The service starts the oldest waiting verification after arrivals and consumes one unit of its two-unit service requirement at each action-start boundary. Completion therefore occurs after two service boundaries and applies the result before the next action. A packet's validation queue wait is the number of boundaries from its arrival to its service start. The remaining budget and queue state are public only after their event has occurred.

At every slot the simulator performs this exact sequence: (1) process successful arrivals, enqueue verification packets or expose metadata, start/advance service and apply completed graph corrections; expire candidates whose boundary is strictly beyond generation plus `10`; (2) read all action records; (3) create metadata and verification requests and sort/admit them; (4) evaluate admitted channel samples; (5) resolve the four proposed moves in robot order and charge motion and admitted-payload energy; (6) create post-motion observations and metadata payloads; (7) schedule successful arrivals, update candidate/packet bookkeeping and add the deterministic drift increment; (8) record boundary `t+1`. At terminal boundary `80`, arrivals and service are processed and uncompleted candidates/packets are closed as dropped; no action is read.

Movement is resolved in robot order. A valid non-stay move whose destination is outside the grid, inside an obstacle, already accepted by a lower-numbered robot, or equal to a higher robot's action-start cell is replaced by `stay` and increments `safety_events` once. A malformed move is a legality failure and never increments that counter. A valid move costs `1`, a stay or safety replacement costs `0.2`; an admitted payload unit costs `0.05` energy. Energy starts at `100` and may not fall below `-1e-6`. At the end of each slot, for each axis use the first 16 hexadecimal digits of SHA-256 of `scenario|variant|r<robot>|t<slot>|drift|<axis>` divided by `2^64`, and add `c*(1+0.2v)*m_i*(2u-1)` to drift, where `c` is the scenario coefficient and `m_i` is the heterogeneous multiplier (or `1`).

The preceding sequence is authoritative. Decision fields describe requests at the action boundary. Metadata, candidates, packet outcomes, service, verification, drift, graph, safety and mission values are post-event evidence derived by the fixed model. No future event is available to a decision.

#### Legal actions and boundaries

The JSON action fields must have the declared types and enums. A non-null `validate_edge` must be an unexpired, not-yet-requested edge visible at that action-start boundary. Requests that do not fit channel capacity, remaining budget or the verification queue are environment admission outcomes and are not silently converted into another candidate action; malformed fields, invisible/expired/repeated edge references and energy below `-1e-6` are `decision_legality` failures. Valid but unsafe moves are replay safety events, not legality failures. An edge that is generated but never requested expires and counts as dropped; a verification request rejected by channel capacity or remaining budget, a packet that fails its link, or a packet rejected on arrival also counts as dropped. All fixed hashes and thresholds are evaluated only after the corresponding decision.

#### Task metrics

All metrics are calculated from fixed replay, clipped to `[0,1]`, and never read from a candidate report or self-reported summary. For each robot, `arrival` is the first boundary at which its true cell equals its goal. `primary_metrics_1` gives `1` by the deadline, gives `0.5*clip(1-(arrival-deadline)/max(1,80-deadline),0,1)` when later, and otherwise gives `0.5*clip(1-shortest_distance(final,goal)/max(1,shortest_distance(start,goal)),0,1)`; it averages robots and variants. `primary_metrics_2` is `0.6*clip(1-pose_MSE/22^2,0,1)+0.4*clip(1-graph_residual/16,0,1)`, where `pose_MSE` averages squared drift norms over all `4*81` boundaries, `graph_residual` is the arithmetic mean of the residuals of all graph edges present at boundary `80`, and empty graph residual is `16`. `primary_metrics_3` is `0.65*clip(1-safety_events/(80*4),0,1)+0.35*clip(1-motion_cost/(80*4),0,1)`. `auxiliary_metrics_1` is `0.5*clip(1-total_exchange_units/640,0,1)+0.5*informative_units/max(1,total_exchange_units)`, with informative units equal to the payload units of a first correct closure or first fixed-hash rejection of an inconsistent edge. `auxiliary_metrics_2` is `0.5*clip(1-validation_wait_slots/80,0,1)+0.5*clip(1-dropped_or_expired_candidates/max(1,total_candidates),0,1)`; when no candidate exists, its second term is `1`. The normalized task performance is `(8*primary_metrics_1+4*primary_metrics_2+3*primary_metrics_3+3*auxiliary_metrics_1+2*auxiliary_metrics_2)/20`.

Each `actions` array has exactly `80*4=320` unique `(slot,robot)` records and no other fields. `slot` is a JSON integer in `0..79`, `robot` is one of `r0..r3`, `move` is one of `stay,N,E,S,W`, `broadcast_metadata` is a JSON boolean, and `validate_edge` is `null` or the exact public edge ID visible at the action start. Record order is irrelevant. Positions, observations, candidates, packets, probabilities, queue/service events, validation results, graph edges, mission state and metric values are not candidate fields. The grader uses finite arithmetic and fixed internal tolerance `1e-6`.
