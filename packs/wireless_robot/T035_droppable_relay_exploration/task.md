## Prompt

### Scenario

Four exploration robots must map a long, obstructed site while keeping a remote base informed. Each robot can carry two small radio nodes and can leave a node behind as a static relay. The robots move through a grid whose geometry creates narrow routes and intermittent wireless links. They generate map, critical-report, and alarm packets while exploring. Packets compete for a shared hop budget, can expire in a local queue, and may need a one-slot retry after a failed end-to-end transmission.

The team must balance exploration and communication. A robot that travels beyond the current relay chain may discover valuable cells but leave critical reports disconnected. A relay placed too close to another relay wastes scarce deployment capacity and can increase interference. A full packet preserves more information but consumes twice as many queue and link units as a compact packet. The base only acknowledges a packet after the selected direct or multi-relay route succeeds at the next boundary.

### Research Problem

Build a deterministic simulator and design one unified, state-driven method for all fifteen canonical scenario variants. At every action boundary, choose a legal move for every robot, whether one carried node is dropped, the encoding of the packet generated by that robot after the move, and at most one queued packet plus an ordered static-relay path to the base. The method should use the current team state, queue ages, report gaps, deployed-node geometry, link history, exploration value, and remaining energy to trade off deadline-qualified coverage, critical-report delivery, connectivity, safety, deployment cost, queue age, and hop load.

The method, approximations, search procedure, parameters, and project organization are your choices. The fixed scenarios and variants are evaluation inputs, not permission to provide scenario-indexed routes, drop schedules, packet schedules, or result rows. Additional controlled experiments are welcome when they leave the canonical result contract unchanged.

#### State, Actions, and Task Model

At action-start boundary `t`, each robot has a position, energy, two carried-node slots, last-drop slot, and a local queue of packet records. The coordinate increments are fixed as `N:(0,+1)`, `E:(+1,0)`, `S:(0,-1)`, `W:(-1,0)`, and `stay:(0,0)`. The initial energy is `100`, both node slots are available, all queues are empty, and no node is deployed; at boundary `0`, the four start cells are already visited and have `first_visit=0`. Deployed nodes are static and have IDs `r<i>d<j>`, where `j=0,1` is the order in which robot `r<i>` drops its nodes. A node becomes available from boundary `t+1` after a drop at slot `t`.

Each action object contains `move`, `drop`, `encode`, `send_packet`, and `relay_path`. `move` is one of `stay`, `N`, `E`, `S`, `W`. `drop` is a JSON boolean. `encode` is `compact` or `full` and selects the packet encoding generated after this robot's movement. `send_packet` is either `null` or one packet ID currently in that robot's action-start queue. `relay_path` is an ordered array of zero, one, or two deployed-node IDs; it must be empty when `send_packet` is null, while a non-null packet may use the empty path for direct delivery or one/two relays. The route is the sender, the listed relays in order, and the fixed base. A drop requires `move=stay`, `send_packet=null`, and an empty path; a drop cannot transmit in the same slot.

At the end of every slot, each robot generates one packet for the cell it occupies at boundary `t+1`. Its packet ID is `scenario=<name>|variant=<v>|robot=<i>|slot=<t>`. Let `z=(t+v) mod 12`: `z=0..5` generates `map`, `z=6..8` generates `critical`, and `z=9..11` generates `alarm`. In `critical_burst`, slots `20+5v..31+5v` generate `critical` regardless of `z`. A packet has class limits and class weights `map:(12,1)`, `critical:(8,4)`, and `alarm:(4,3)`, where the first value is its maximum age and the second its weight. If the robot is the recorded owner of an unacknowledged report region and occupies that region when the packet is generated, the grader automatically associates the packet with that report ID; the candidate does not submit a separate report-ID field. Repeated packets by that owner while it remains in the region can carry the same report. If multiple robots first enter an unacknowledged region in one slot, the lowest robot ID owns that observation, and only that owner creates report-bearing packets while occupying the region until the report is acknowledged. A packet generated after the owner leaves the region is not associated with that report unless the owner later re-enters it. The packet's `compact` encoding is `(payload_units, retained_fraction)=(1,0.60)` and `full` is `(2,1.00)`.

Each robot queue has capacity `12` payload units. At every boundary, packets whose age is strictly greater than their class limit are removed before the next action; expiry is checked before retry. After a slot's retry/drop outcomes, newly generated packets are added and the queue retains a prefix ordered by `(effective deadline, class weight descending, generation slot, packet ID)`, where `effective deadline` is generation slot plus class maximum age; packets are retained whole until the capacity is exhausted. A packet that is successfully delivered is acknowledged at the next boundary and removed from its queue. A packet whose first route attempt fails may be requested only in the immediately following slot with the identical packet ID and identical `relay_path`; if it is not requested, is not admitted, or fails again, it is dropped at that boundary.

The fixed grid and all current state are deterministic. A route request costs `payload_units * (len(relay_path)+1)` hop-units and must fit entirely in the remaining per-slot capacity. There are at most `8` admitted hop-units per slot and at most `720=8H` over one scenario variant. A successful route delivers the packet to the base at boundary `t+1` only when every leg succeeds. No relay queue is submitted: relay forwarding is the fixed same-slot second or third leg of the selected route.

For a leg, adjacent endpoints must have Manhattan distance at most `10`, and each deployed relay must have a strictly smaller shortest traversable distance to the base than its predecessor; relay IDs cannot repeat. A shortest traversable distance uses only four-neighbor `N/E/S/W` moves through traversable cells. Chebyshev distance is `max(|dx|,|dy|)`. Let `d` be Manhattan distance divided by `10`, clipped to `[0,1]`, `o=1` when shortest traversable distance is greater than Manhattan distance, and `l` be the number of other deployed nodes within Chebyshev distance `1` of the exact leg midpoint, excluding leg endpoints. Let `n` be the number of requests admitted in the slot. The link probability is:

```text
p = clip(0.96 - 0.28*d - 0.10*o - 0.04*(n-1) - 0.025*l - i, 0.08, 0.96)
```

Here `i=0.08` for a `split_tunnel` leg midpoint with `x>=12`; `i=0.16` when an `interference_shift` leg midpoint is within Chebyshev distance `2` of `(4+(t+7v) mod 18,7)`; otherwise `i=0`. For each leg, `hop_id` is `<scenario>|<variant>|<packet_id>|a<attempt>|g<leg>|<sender>|<receiver>`. The sample is the big-endian `uint64` obtained from the first eight SHA256 digest bytes (equivalently, the first sixteen hexadecimal characters) of `UTF-8(hop_id+"|salt="+str(11*v))`, divided by `2^64`; the leg succeeds exactly when the sample is less than `p`. The sample is revealed only after the corresponding action; future samples may not be used to choose an action.

#### Information Availability and Event Order

The unified decision procedure receives the complete action-start state: all robot positions and energies, deployed-node positions and IDs, carried-node counts and drop history, every robot queue and packet metadata, unacknowledged reports, coverage and report ownership, previous actions, previous admission decisions, and link outcomes already revealed by replay. It knows all fixed scenario geometry, schedules, deadlines, capacities, and formulas. It does not receive future hash samples, future actions, future queue results, future deployments, or future coverage/report outcomes. A deterministic forecast based on visible history is allowed; precomputing a scenario-, variant-, robot-, packet-, or absolute-slot-indexed action table is not.

At each slot the grader applies this order:

1. At the action-start boundary, remove over-age packets and expose ACKs and all other current state.
2. Read the five decision fields for all four robots and validate local references, drop constraints, paths, and energy.
3. Rank complete packet-route requests by `(effective deadline, retry first, generation slot, robot order, packet ID)` and admit complete requests while eight hop-units remain.
4. Evaluate every admitted leg using action-start positions, deployed nodes, the fixed probability formula, and its canonical hash sample.
5. Resolve proposed movement in robot order `r0,r1,r2,r3`, replacing unsafe destinations by stay and counting each replaced nonzero move as one safety event.
6. Apply energy costs, route outcomes, retry/drop state, and base ACKs. A successful packet is available as an acknowledged result at boundary `t+1`.
7. Apply valid drops at the post-motion cell, generate and queue each robot's encoded packet for boundary `t+1`, retain the queue prefix, and then update coverage and report ownership in robot order at the post-motion cells. A newly visited region therefore affects packets generated in a later slot, and a packet is report-bearing only when its recorded owner occupies that region at generation.

Decision fields describe candidate actions at the action boundary. Link samples, admitted requests, packet paths, ACKs, queue snapshots, node availability, positions after safety replacement, coverage, reports, safety events, and all metrics are execution results derived by the grader.

#### Legal Actions and Boundaries

The only legal move values are `stay,N,E,S,W`; malformed values fail `decision_legality`. A proposed destination outside the grid, in an obstacle, on an already accepted lower-order destination, or on the action-start position of a higher-order robot is replaced by stay and counted as a safety event. Pairwise swaps therefore leave both robots in place. Movement is not itself an invalid decision when the proposed destination is unsafe.

`drop=true` requires stay, an empty send, a valid carried node, a traversable post-motion cell, no existing deployed node on that cell, and at least six slots since that robot's preceding drop. The robot may drop at most two nodes. A non-null `send_packet` must be in the sender's action-start queue, and its path must be an ordered list of distinct currently deployed node IDs satisfying the route distance and strict base-progress rules. A null send requires an empty path. The packet encoding is always one of the two public values; it affects only the newly generated packet at the end of the slot. A request that does not fit remaining capacity is an environment outcome, not an illegal action. A malformed packet reference, route, drop, or resulting negative energy is a legality failure.

Each action's energy is recomputed from the fixed event order with tolerance `1e-6`: stay costs `0.2`, an effective task move costs `1`, a valid drop costs `8`, and each admitted hop-unit costs `0.05` charged to the sending robot (static relays have no energy account). Safety replacement still incurs stay cost. Invalid actions do not receive performance safety credit; valid actions that cause runtime safety replacement, queue drops, packet expiry, retries, or disconnection are replay outcomes.

#### Task Metrics

All metrics are computed independently from replay and clipped to `[0,1]`. For every traversable cell, `best_fraction` is the maximum retained fraction among packets generated while a robot occupied that cell and delivered to the base by that cell's fixed deadline; it is zero when no such packet exists. `primary_metrics_1` is the value-weighted mean of `best_fraction` over the full traversable grid, averaged over fifteen variants.

For each of the eight report regions, the best acknowledged report arrival is scored `1` when it arrives by its table deadline, `0.5*clip(1-(arrival-deadline)/max(1,90-deadline),0,1)` when later, and `0` when no report arrives. `primary_metrics_2` is the report-value-weighted mean over regions and variants.

For one variant, `progress` is the value of cells first visited by their cell deadline divided by total traversable-cell value. `disconnected_required_slots` counts slots in which at least one unacknowledged report packet exists but no holder has a legal zero-to-two-relay route at action start. The variant's `primary_metrics_3` is `0.45*progress + 0.30*clip(1-disconnected_required_slots/90,0,1) + 0.25*clip(1-safety_events/(90*4),0,1)`, averaged over variants.

For `auxiliary_metrics_1`, the fixed denominator is the total class weight of the `90*4` generated opportunities. The numerator is class weight times retained fraction for packets delivered by their class maximum age. `mean_queue_age` is the mean, over all `91*4` robot-boundary snapshots, of the payload-unit-weighted age of each queue; an empty queue contributes zero. The value is `0.5*class_service_fraction + 0.5*clip(1-mean_queue_age/12,0,1)`. `auxiliary_metrics_2` is `0.5*clip(1-deployed_nodes/8,0,1) + 0.5*clip(1-accepted_hop_units/720,0,1)`.

The normalized task performance is `(8*primary_metrics_1 + 4*primary_metrics_2 + 3*primary_metrics_3 + 3*auxiliary_metrics_1 + 2*auxiliary_metrics_2)/20`. The grader derives all denominators, values, paths, and event counts from the fixed model and validated decisions; candidate-reported metric values are never read.
